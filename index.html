EXPLORE THE ARCHIVE FOR ARTISTS

When I was young, I could put on a record, sit down with the album cover, and disappear.

For a little while, the room I was sitting in didn’t matter anymore.

I was somewhere else.

The music, the artwork, the photographs, the words on the sleeve — all of it belonged together. A record wasn’t just something playing in the background.

It was a place you could go.

Somewhere along the way, we lost a lot of that.

Music became something we scroll past, skip through, shuffle, and squeeze between everything else demanding our attention.

The songs are still there.

But the world around them is disappearing.

I miss that world.

And I don’t think it has to stay gone.

You made a record.
Where does it live?

Streaming gave people a way to hear it.
Social media gave you a way to promote it.

LPX gives the record itself a place to live.

A place where the music stays at the center, while everything that belongs with it has room to exist — artwork, lyrics, photographs, video, notes, stories, a graphic novel, an interactive world, or something nobody has thought of yet.

It can be as simple or as ambitious as the record wants to be.

LPX doesn’t tell you what your album should become.

You do.
